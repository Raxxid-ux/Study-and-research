Module 01

Foundations, History & Nature vs. Nurture Interplay

Beyond the "Versus" Dichotomy

Psychology is the scientific study of the mind, the brain, and how they drive behavior. Cognitive psychology rejects pure biological determinism and pure blank-slate empiricism: your genes build possibilities, but your environment shapes how those genes manifest.

GENOTYPE

The exact DNA code you inherit from biological parents (the underlying biological blueprint).

PHENOTYPE

What actually gets expressed—observable physical traits, behavioral quirks, and cognitive abilities.

HERITABILITY

A statistical estimate of how much population variance in a trait is driven by genetic variance versus environment.

TWIN & ADOPTION STUDIES

Identical (MZ, 100% shared genes) vs. Fraternal (DZ, 50% shared genes). Separates genetic variance from home rearing.

Shared vs. Non-Shared Environmental Variance
Shared Environment: Household income, parenting styles, family diet, neighborhood.
Non-Shared Environment: Unique friend circles, distinct teachers, personal illness/accidents. Empirically shown to have stronger power in making siblings distinct!

Early Roots, Philosophical Debates & Major Schools

Ancient Roots

Aristotle placed the mind in the heart; Plato linked it to the brain. In 900 CE, physician Avicenna pioneered early empirical studies on memory deficits and psychiatric conditions.

Structuralism (Wilhelm Wundt)

Set up first formal psychology lab. Sought to break conscious thought into micro-components using systematic, trained introspection.

Functionalism (William James)

Argued the conscious mind is a flowing stream that cannot be dissected. Focuses on why mental processes evolve to aid survival and adaptation.

Psychoanalysis (Sigmund Freud)

Consciousness is merely the tip of the iceberg; behavior is steered by deep unconscious drives, conflicts, and childhood trauma.

Mind-Body Debates & The Computer Metaphor

Dualism (mind and body are separate entities) vs. Mind-Brain Reductionism (mental states are pure physical neural activity). The challenge: explaining how raw electrical voltage translates into subjective qualitative experiences (qualia).

Computer Metaphor: Brain = silicon hardware; Mind = software algorithms. You cannot fully comprehend how an operating system runs merely by measuring current in silicon!

Module 02

How We Study the Brain: Methods & Neuroimaging

1

Lesion Studies

Inspect deficits when specific areas are damaged (e.g., Broca's aphasia). Limited by messy damage and post-injury brain plasticity.

2

Functional Recording

Measure metabolic or electrical signals during tasks (fMRI BOLD effect, EEG electrical microvolts, MEG magnetic fields).

3

Structural Anatomy

Examine morphology and tissue density using CT scans (X-ray density) or MRI (hydrogen proton radiofrequencies).

4

Neural Stimulation

TMS wands apply magnetic pulses to non-invasively activate or switch off cortical patches, proving direct causality.

Module 03

Human Language: Acquisition & Comprehension

Stages of Speech & Syntax Development

0 to 48 Months
<12m
Universal Listener & Babbling Stage Phonemes

Infants discriminate almost every phoneme across global languages. By 10-12 months, phonetic tuning discards non-native sounds. Babbling starts with vowels, progressing to consonant-vowel combinations ("ba-ba", "ma-ma").

12-26m
Telegraphic Stage (Two-Word Syntax)

Concise 2-word constructions lacking grammatical inflections or helper words (e.g., "doggy go", "more milk"). High semantic information density.

27-30m
Grammatical Morphemes & Overregularization

Children attach plurals, past tenses, and questions. They display overregularization ("I goed outside", "two feets"), proving they construct internal algorithmic rules rather than pure parrot-like mimicry!

35-46m
Complex Hierarchical Sentences

Embedding subordinate clauses, conjunctions, and logical conditionals ("The boy who took my truck is going away").

Module 04

Working Memory & Sensory Perception

Baddeley & Hitch Tripartite Working Memory Model

Parallel Systems

Memory is not a passive bottleneck (as proved by Patient KF, whose verbal short-term span was damaged to 1-2 items, yet long-term storage stayed intact).

Central Executive

Attentional supervisor, coordinates subsystems, strategy selection, cognitive task-switching.

Phonological Loop "The Inner Voice & Ear"

Holds acoustic/speech info. Rehearsal loop; vulnerable to word-length effect.

Visuo-Spatial Sketchpad "The Inner Eye"

Manipulates mental imagery, spatial routes, matrix rotation tasks.

The Dual-Task Paradigm Rule

You can navigate a walking path (visuospatial) while listening to an audio lecture (phonological) with minimal friction. But try reading a legal document while someone recites numbers: both collide in the phonological loop, causing catastrophic interference!

Visual Processing: The Two Stream Hypothesis

Select a visual pathway to simulate its cortical route and associated clinical lesion syndrome.

Ventral Stream: Object & Face Identification ("What")

Processes shape, color, texture, and semantic identity. Enables recognizing that an object is a telephone, a cup, or a friend's face.

Lesion Syndrome: Visual Agnosia (cannot name seen objects) or Prosopagnosia (face blindness; can see eyes and nose but cannot identify who it is).
Tonotopic Map: Cochlea & auditory cortex are organized like piano keys by pitch frequency.
Retinotopic Map: Visual cortex neurons maintain a direct 2D spatial map of the retina.
Module 05

Attention & Consciousness: How We Experience Reality

Taxonomy of Attention & The Attentional Spotlight

Top-Down (Voluntary)

Goal-directed, conscious focal search (e.g., searching for keys).

Bottom-Up (Involuntary)

Salience-driven (e.g., sudden screeching tire or flash of red).

Selective vs Sustained

Filtering out cocktail party chatter vs. maintaining vigilance over hours.

Effortless Flow

High task engagement without subjective strain (expert musicians).

Hemispatial Neglect vs. Inattentional Blindness
Inattentional Blindness: Missing a visible unexpected object (e.g., invisible gorilla) because focus is totally allocated to another task.
Hemispatial Neglect: Damage to right posterior parietal cortex causing inability to orient attention to the contralateral (left) space. Not blindness—an attentional orientation deficit!
Attention Without Consciousness:

Subliminal masked primes capture spatial attention without subjective awareness. "Zombie behaviors" (smoothly cycling, tying laces) execute without conscious scrutiny.

High-Yield Synthesis

Quick Revision Sheet & Key Distinctions

GENOTYPE vs PHENOTYPE

Genotype: The unexpressed genetic blueprint code.
Phenotype: The actual observable biological and behavioral expression.

OVEREXTENSION vs UNDEREXTENSION

Overextension: Calling all four-legged animals "doggy".
Underextension: Believing "cat" only denotes the specific family pet.

APHASIA vs AMUSIA

Double Dissociation: Brain damage can impair speech while music stays intact (aphasia), or destroy music perception while speech is spared (amusia).

INATTENTIONAL BLINDNESS vs NEGLECT

Inattentional: Missing stimulus due to full task loading.
Neglect: Inability to attend to opposite space due to parietal stroke.
